# clearSky — faceless TikTok playbook

Solo-founder, organic only. No one on camera. Every format below is a screen recording of a real clearSky surface plus text-on-screen, optional voiceover and B-roll you film or license. All of PLAN §1 (ethics) and §7 (aviation "do not say / say instead") apply to every caption, voiceover line and on-screen word.

## 1. Positioning (one line)

**The calm, honest co-pilot for nervous flyers: real sounds explained, real instruments, no fake scores.**

Tone: validate, then inform. Second person, short sentences, no exclamation marks, no "just relax". Say "bumps". Never promise the viewer will stop being afraid.

## 2. Content formats (8)

Record in dark mode, 9:16, with the app's large-type surfaces. Hide the status bar clock/battery, or use a clean device status. Use a Do Not Disturb profile so no notifications leak. Campaign token = the `ct` value for App Store Connect (see §8).

### F1 — "What's that sound?" (the workhorse; 3 posts/week)
- **Length:** 12–20 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) B-roll: runway or wing at takeoff, 2 s. (2) Screen: Sounds list scrolling, stop on the entry. (3) Screen: the entry card, hold 4 s on "what it is". (4) Screen: "why it's normal". (5) End card: app icon, "search clearSky on the App Store".
- **On-screen text:** "That thump after takeoff" → "It's the landing gear folding away" → "Right on schedule." → "More in clearSky".
- **Voiceover (optional):** "That thump after takeoff is the landing gear folding into the aircraft. It happens on every flight."
- **Entries to cycle (all in the app):** gear thump, flaps whirr, engines seeming to go quiet after takeoff, double ding, "barking dog" PTU noise (A320 family), ear pressure, speed-brake rumble, reverse thrust on landing, APU hum, fuel pump sounds, wing flex, go-around.

### F2 — "What a bump actually reads" (2 posts/week)
- **Length:** 15–25 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) G-meter dial and slow trace, hold. (2) Hand taps the phone or phone on a car dashboard on a bumpy road, showing the dial move a little. (3) Certification card: limit manoeuvring load, "tested well beyond", citation visible.
- **On-screen text:** "That bump felt huge" → "Your phone reads about this" → "Aircraft are built for far more (14 CFR 25.337)".
- **Rules:** this shows the phone's reading, not the aircraft's, and the app says so. If not recorded on a real flight, add a "Demo" or "Phone on a car dashboard" tag on screen. Never say the number is "what planes feel". No peak-hold or red zones.

### F3 — "Your flight in 9 phases" (1–2 posts/week)
- **Length:** 20–30 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) Timeline animating from "taxi out" to "taxi in". (2) Pause on "takeoff" with "what you'll hear and feel next". (3) Pause on "descent" for the same. (4) Wheels-down warm sunrise wash.
- **On-screen text:** "9 phases. Every flight." → "Taxi · Takeoff · Climb · Cruise · Descent · Landing" → "Here's what each one sounds like." → "Labelled 'estimated', because it is."

### F4 — "Breathe out with me" (1–2 posts/week)
- **Length:** 30–60 s (the ring is the video).
- **Shot list:** (1) Breathing ring, full-screen, one full cycle with silence. (2) Optional haptic-style visual cue (a soft pulse overlay). (3) Slow B-roll cloud loop for the last 5 s.
- **On-screen text:** "Breathe in for 4." / "Out for 6." (matching the pacing) → "Longer out-breath helps the body settle." (source: NHS, cited in-app) → "Try it again in the app."
- **Rules:** extended exhale or coherent only. Never 4-7-8, never long holds, never "deep breaths". Do not frame as treating anxiety or panic. No "this will stop a panic attack".

### F5 — "Weather report, translated" (2 posts/week)
- **Length:** 20–30 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) A raw public report line (use the bundled fixture or a real recent public PIREP). (2) Outlook screen scrolling to the plain-language translation. (3) The "Some bumps likely" band, then the limits statement.
- **On-screen text:** "UA /OV … /TB LGT" → "Light turbulence reported at that altitude" → "Reports are snapshots. The air changes."
- **Rules:** label the data "public NOAA report" with the timestamp. Never imply a forecast guarantee. Never show a score or percentage (the app has none).

### F6 — "Flight #N landed" (1 post/week; rewards, not guilt)
- **Length:** 10–15 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) Card "prints" with the one haptic (visual ease-in). (2) Route arc hold, date visible, fear numbers off. (3) Card rendered over a licensed window-seat B-roll.
- **On-screen text:** "Flight #3" → "Still nervous. Still boarded." → "Make yours in clearSky."
- **Rules:** use only your own test data; no invented flyers or "user stories". Never show a number of "people who flew".

### F7 — "Wrong vs. right" myth swap (1 post/week)
- **Length:** 15–20 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) Text "Air pockets" struck through. (2) Learn lesson screen. (3) Sources footer visible. (4) End card.
- **On-screen text (use only rows from the PLAN §7 table):** "Not 'air pockets'" → "It's moving air" · "Planes don't drop thousands of feet — bumps are a few feet to tens of feet" · "Radar shows rain, not clear-air bumps — pilots use forecasts and reports too".
- **Rules:** use the "say instead" column verbatim in spirit; every fact needs a source in `docs/CONTENT_SOURCES.md` marked verified before it appears in a post.

### F8 — "At the gate" reset (1 post/week)
- **Length:** 15–20 s.
- **Shot list:** (1) Gate B-roll or generic waiting-area B-roll with no faces. (2) SOS ring opening (first seconds only). (3) 5-4-3-2-1 grounding step screen.
- **On-screen text:** "Heart racing at the gate?" → "Name 5 things you can see" → "If it feels like more than nerves, tell the crew or a doctor."
- **Rules:** grounding is a technique, not a treatment. Always include the professional/crew pointer.

## 3. Hook lines (first 1–2 seconds, on-screen text, 30)

Calm, validating, then promise information. None promise a cure or a percent.

1. That thump after takeoff? Here's what it is.
2. The engines went quiet after takeoff. That's normal.
3. That "ding" before the seatbelt sign? Here's what it means.
4. Bumps feel huge. Here's what your phone actually reads.
5. Nervous flyer? You're not the only one in row 24.
6. Hear a whirr under the wing? Flaps. Here's why.
7. Why your ears do that when you land.
8. Your flight, in nine phases, sound by sound.
9. If your heart races at the gate, try this slow breath.
10. Breathe out longer than you breathe in. Try one with me.
11. A weather report for nervous flyers, in plain English.
12. "UA /TB MOD" means this. Translated.
13. No, there are no "pockets" in the air. Here's what it is.
14. Turbulence feels dramatic. Here's what the plane is built for.
15. What the pilot's announcement actually means.
16. Booked a flight and dreading it? One small thing to try tonight.
17. The "barking dog" noise on some planes? It has a name.
18. Why the lights dim for landing.
19. The wing is flexing. It's meant to.
20. Why the plane feels like it's turning around after takeoff.
21. Landing felt rough? Here's what happened.
22. A calm walk through takeoff, step by step.
23. What a go-around is, and why pilots do them.
24. Your seat belt sign is on. Here's what that's for.
25. Anxious before a trip? You can plan for that.
26. How to read your own flight's weather, without the scary parts.
27. A grounding exercise you can do in seat 14C.
28. The loudest sound on landing, explained.
29. A flight app that shows its sources.
30. Fear of flying doesn't mean something is wrong with you.

Hooks 5, 16, 25, 30 are validation-first (best for save/share). Hooks 1–3, 6, 17, 18, 28 are the most search-like. Rotate; never reuse the same hook within 14 days.

## 4. Four-week posting calendar

Cadence: **5 posts/week** (Mon, Tue, Thu, Fri, Sun). Batch-record two evenings a week (about 3 hours total) plus one editing block. Post 6–8 pm local (when people plan trips) and test one mid-morning slot in week 3.

| Week | Mon | Tue | Thu | Fri | Sun | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | F1 gear thump | F3 phases | F1 double ding | F4 breathe | F7 "air pockets" | Establish the three signature visuals |
| 2 | F1 engines quiet | F5 translator | F2 g-meter | F1 flaps | F8 at the gate | Add trust formats (F2, F5) |
| 3 | F3 phases pt.2 (descent) | F1 PTU "barking dog" | F5 translator | F4 breathe | F6 card | Test posting time; double down on top format |

Mix by week 4: F1 ~35%, F5 ~20%, F2/F3/F4 ~15% each, F6/F7/F8 fill. Pin the best F1 and the best F5. Reply to comments within 24 hours with video replies (screen-record the answer).

**Measure each week (TikTok Analytics + App Store Connect):** average watch time, completion rate, saves and shares per format (saves matter most), profile visits, clicks on the bio link, plus App Store Connect product page views and first-time downloads per campaign link. See §8.

## 5. Captions and hashtags

- **Caption structure (≤ 150 characters):** one honest sentence (what the video showed) + one soft line pointing to the app. Example: "The thump after takeoff is the landing gear. Full explainer in clearSky. Search clearSky on the App Store."
- **Hashtags: 3–5 only.** Always one of `#fearofflying` / `#flightanxiety`; one topic tag (`#turbulence`, `#planesounds`, `#aviation`); optionally `#nervousflyer`, `#flyingtips`. Not `#cure`, not `#therapy`, not `#anxietyhack`, no competitor names, no tags unrelated to flying to chase reach.
- **Keywords in the on-screen text and the spoken first line** (TikTok search reads them): "fear of flying", "turbulence", "takeoff sounds".
- **Voice:** if you use a synthetic voice, turn on TikTok's AI-generated content label. Music: use TikTok's Commercial Music Library (business-account safe) or royalty-free tracks, never trending commercial songs.
- **Legal/safety line in the profile bio:** "Self-help wellness tool. Not medical advice."
- **Bio:** "Calm, honest help for nervous flyers. Search clearSky on the App Store." + one link (see §8).

## 6. Comment-reply templates

Warm, short, never diagnostic, never "you'll be fine", never statistics. Adapt, do not copy-paste the same text twice in a day.

| Situation | Reply |
|---|---|
| "I have a flight tomorrow and I'm terrified" | "That sounds really hard. Tonight, pick one small thing: lay out your documents and a breathing track. On the plane, tell the cabin crew you're nervous; they help with this all the time." |
| "I had a panic attack on my last flight" | "I'm sorry, that's scary. If this keeps happening, a doctor or a qualified therapist can really help. On board, tell the crew. Slow, longer out-breaths can be a small thing to try in the moment." |
| "Does this really work?" | "I can't promise results. It's a self-help tool that explains things and gives you breathing and grounding practice. Some people find it steadies them. If your fear is large, please also talk to a professional." |
| "What was that noise/bump?" | "[One factual sentence from the app, with the source]. If something ever worries you in the air, the crew can tell you exactly what's happening." |
| "Planes still crash" | "It's okay to have that fear. I won't throw statistics at you, because numbers don't calm fear. What helps some people is learning what each sound and phase is. That's what we cover." |
| "I'm scared to even book" | "That's very common and nothing to be ashamed of. A small first step could be reading about one phase of flight. Whenever you're ready, no pressure." |
| Comment about chest pain, fainting, trouble breathing | "Please treat that as medical. Tell the crew straight away, or on the ground call your local emergency number." |
| Comment about wanting to hurt themselves | "I'm really sorry you're hurting. ## 7. CTA rules

- The only CTA is **"search clearSky on the App Store"** (spoken and on-screen on the last 2 seconds). Same wording in the caption.
- No "link in bio" as the main CTA, no "download now", no "limited time", no countdown, no "before it's gone", no fake discounts, no "free" (there is no free tier).
- No "trust me", "everyone is using", "users love", "as seen on", user counts, ratings or reviews, even if true; the ethics rules ban them in marketing copy.
- Never say the app "treats", "helps cure", "reduces anxiety by X percent". Allowed: "explains", "guides", "helps you practise breathing", "helps you feel prepared".
- One CTA per video, last 2 seconds only. Never in the first 5 seconds.
- Mention price only if asked (use the reply above) and only as shown in the App Store.

## 8. Attribution (App Store Connect campaign links, no SDK)

**Goal:** know which format sends people to the App Store, without any tracking inside the app. No analytics SDK, no "how did you hear about us".

**Setup (one time, 15 minutes):**
1. App Store Connect > your app > **Analytics** > **Sources** > **Campaigns** > **Create campaign link** (or use `https://apps.apple.com/app/id<APP_ID>?pt=<provider_token>&ct=<campaign>&mt=8`).
2. Create 8 links, one per format, with these campaign names (the `ct` value, ≤ 100 chars): `tt_sounds`, `tt_gmeter`, `tt_phases`, `tt_breathe`, `tt_outlook`, `tt_card`, `tt_myth`, `tt_gate`. Optional: add a seventh for `tt_bio_default` for general profile traffic.
3. For each format, create a **Custom Product Page** (see `docs/APP_STORE.md` §13), and use the link that includes its `ppid`.
4. TikTok allows **one clickable bio link**. Rotate it weekly to the campaign link of that week's top format, or use a link-in-bio page you control that is just a list of format links (no tracking scripts). Keep the on-screen CTA "search clearSky on the App Store" because many viewers will search rather than click.
5. Keep a one-sheet log (date, format, hook number, bio link in effect).

**Reading the results (weekly, Fridays):**
- App Store Connect > Analytics > Sources: per campaign, **Impressions → Product Page Views → First-time downloads** and conversion rate. Compare formats on downloads per 1,000 profile visits (TikTok shows profile views).
- Compare **App Store Search** first-time downloads on weeks with high video reach versus low. Search-driven installs are your hidden TikTok effect and will not appear under any campaign.
- Sales & Trends and Subscription reports show total new subscribers and conversions by territory; campaign-level paid conversion is **not available** without an SDK. Estimate per-format paid value as (downloads from that campaign) × (your overall download-to-paid rate for the week).
- Caveats (be honest with yourself): Apple counts campaign data only for users who share analytics data, so numbers are an undercount; sample sizes are small, so judge on 3-week trends, never one post; do not chase tiny differences.
- Decision rule after week 4: keep the 4 formats with the best downloads per 1,000 profile visits plus the best save rate; retire the bottom two.

## 9. Conversion notes: onboarding → paywall (within the rules)

What may influence conversion, without manipulation:
- **Keep onboarding under 2 minutes**; the felt-demo breath is the proof of value. Do not add questions, and do not shorten the 60-second breath to hide it.
- **Show real personalisation**: the plan reveal visibly uses the fear type, peak moment and flight date the user chose. This is honest and the best lever.
- **Paywall clarity**: yearly preselected with the full billed price most prominent, per-week equivalent smaller, weekly equally legible, Restore / Terms / Privacy visible without scrolling, no "free" language unless the store returns a real intro offer.
- **Match the promise**: use App Store Custom Product Pages so the screenshots echo the video viewers watched. Fewer surprise cancellations, fewer refunds.
- **Test with Apple's tools**, not in-app trackers: Product Page Optimization tests (screenshots, icon, promo text) in App Store Connect; one change at a time, 7–14 days.
- **Weekly vs yearly honesty**: never imply savings that are not computed from the two real prices. If you mention the gap, derive it from the store strings and show both.
- **Do not**: add countdown timers, fake "only today" prices, "X people subscribed", guilt copy ("don't give up on your flight"), fake scarcity, confirm-shaming buttons, hidden auto-renew text, or a paywall that can't be reached back from ("Back" must return to the plan reveal).
- **Where to look for problems**: if product-page views are strong but downloads are weak, fix screenshots and subtitle; if downloads are strong but subscriptions are weak, review the paywall's legibility and price clarity in the sandbox on a 6.1" device; do not add pressure.
- **Refunds and cancellations**: honour Apple's process, answer support emails within a day, link "Manage Subscription" prominently in Settings. A trustworthy cancel flow is a marketing asset.

## 10. Never-post list

1. Any person on camera, any face, any stranger's face in B-roll, or identifiable seat-mates (crop or choose angles).
2. Real or invented reviews, testimonials, "users say", star ratings, download counts, subscriber counts, "as seen on", awards.
3. Crash footage, crash audio, accident reconstructions, near-miss clips, in-flight emergency clips, scary turbulence footage meant to shock.
4. Crash odds, "1 in 11 million", "X times safer than driving", "no fatal crash since <year>", "never crashed", "flying is 100% safe".
5. "Cure", "treat", "therapy", "clinically proven", "stops panic attacks", success percentages, "get over your fear in X days".
6. "Air pockets", "planes drop thousands of feet", "autopilot lands the plane", fixed glide distances, wing-bend numbers, "pilots see all turbulence on radar", "doors can't open in flight", "unlimited oxygen" (PLAN §7).
7. Medical, medication or alcohol advice; anything that discourages seeing a professional; anything that tells someone to fly when they are unwell.
8. Countdowns, fake discounts, "limited time", "last chance", guilt or shame ("don't be the one who stays home").
9. Hashtags for competitors, comparisons that name Steady, SOAR, SkyGuru, ReadytoFly or VALK, or screenshots of their apps.
10. The G-meter trace presented as the aircraft's data, or any demo recording that is not labelled "Demo".
11. Invented or unsourced aviation claims; anything not marked `verified` in `docs/CONTENT_SOURCES.md`.
12. Live weather presented as a forecast guarantee; real flight numbers, tail numbers, or someone's itinerary.
13. Asking commenters for health details, collecting contact info in comments or DMs, or using comments to diagnose.
14. Trending audio you do not have rights to; unlabelled synthetic voices.
15. Content that mocks fear, uses jump scares, or ends on an ominous note.
